Trees Pinyin™ Intuitive Phonics System

Complete Technical Specification, Articulatory Mechanisms & Pedagogical Mapping Guide

Part I: Overview & Core Pillars

Trees Pinyin™ is an original English-based phonetic transliteration system invented for native English speakers to master Spoken Mandarin Chinese through natural reading intuition—bypassing IPA and Romanization obstacles.

1. Intuitive Phonics Constructed exclusively from natural English letter combinations and connected speech habits.
2. Silent Guides ( ) Audibly silent letters instruct oral muscles to pre-lock tongue retroflexion or lip rounding.
3. Monosyllabic One-Beat Every Chinese character is uttered in exactly one unified beat using fluent word blending.
4. Contextual Tones Number anchors and the conversational "Do" melody unlock effortless pitch accuracy.
Mandatory Visual Conventions:
  • Bold-Italic Notation: All Trees Pinyin symbols must appear in bold-italics (e.g., booer, (r)s2 hoh4).
  • Uppercase L: Initial l is permanently capitalized as L (e.g., Lah, Leeoh4) to prevent confusion with uppercase I or digit 1.
  • Hyphenated Finals: Finals en, eng, ong use hyphens (er-n, er-ng, er-oong) to prevent italic rn from merging into m.

Part II: Articulatory Muscle Mechanisms

1. The Silent Retroflex Prefix *(r)* (Mandarin zh, ch, sh, r)

Pre-curl the tongue tip backward into the English r posture without vocalizing an r-glide, then immediately release the consonant cluster:

  • (r)ds (zh): Hold r tongue curl + unaspirated ds (as in birds).
  • (r)ts (ch): Hold r tongue curl + strongly aspirated ts (as in cats).
  • (r)s (sh): Hold r tongue curl + release s friction.
  • (r)th (r): Hold r tongue curl + voiced friction th (/ð/, as in this).

2. Silent Labial-Rounding Guide *ee(oo)* (Mandarin ü)

Vocalize the long front vowel ee (/iː/) while keeping lips firmly rounded into an (oo) posture—effortlessly synthesizing authentic Mandarin ü.

3. Non-Rhotic British Vowel Standard *er* & *ar*

Except for the standalone word "Two" (uh-er4 / 二), all er symbols follow British Non-Rhotic rules—the final r is completely flat and silent (as in British her or term).

Part IV: Vowel Omission & Deduplication

When initials tsee (q), see (x), or jee (j) combine with finals starting with ee/i, the redundant ee is omitted automatically:

  • q + ian → tsee + eean → tseean (千, NOT tseeean)
  • x + ing → see + ing → sing (星, NOT seeng)
  • j + ie → jee + eer → jeer (節, NOT jeeeer)

Part V: Tone System & Anchors

Tone 1 (—) & Neutral Omitted. Unmarked syllables naturally speak high & level (doo 都) or soft unstressed.
Tone 2 ( / ) & 3 ( v ) Rising pitch tagged with 2 (doo2 讀); dipping pitch tagged with 3 (doo3 堵).
Tone 4 ( \ ) Emphatic falling tone tagged with 4 (doo4 度, s4 四).
Number-Tone Anchor Mnemonic (e.g. Anchor 12): Mandarin "Twelve" ((r)s2 uh-er4 / 十二 / 12) carries a Tone 2 + Tone 4 contour. "Holiday" (jeer2 (r)th4 / 節日) carries the exact same melody. Learners instantly lock in the pronunciation of Holiday by remembering Twelve Days of Christmas!
